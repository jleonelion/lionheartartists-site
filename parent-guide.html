For Parents

Parent Guide

A clear starting point for families exploring child acting, modeling, commercial, print, television, film, voiceover, and digital creator opportunities.

When a child begins pursuing professional work, parents quickly become the project manager, paperwork keeper, driver, advocate, and calm voice in the room. These are the early pieces families should understand before auditions turn into bookings.

Why a Talent Manager Helps

A strong talent manager gives parents guidance, clarity, and structure. The entertainment industry has moving parts that can feel unfamiliar at first: submissions, auditions, self-tapes, permits, contracts, bookings, set expectations, school needs, and long-term career decisions.

LionHeart helps families understand the process, prepare the right materials, evaluate opportunities, communicate professionally, and protect the child's well-being while the career grows. The goal is not just to chase auditions. It is to manage the full experience around a young performer with care, strategy, and perspective.

Entertainment Work Permit

In California, minors working in entertainment must register for an entertainment work permit through the state. Parents should keep the permit current, track expiration dates, and know that renewals are typically handled every six months. First-time families may also see a short-term permit option through the state system.

Coogan Account

A blocked Coogan trust account protects a portion of a young performer's earnings. Families are commonly asked for account details before or shortly after a booking, and employers may need the account number to deposit the required withheld wages.

School & Set Readiness

Professional work can affect school attendance, tutoring, and set-day schedules. Parents should understand how studio teachers, school approvals, call times, rest periods, and production paperwork fit together before accepting work.

Materials That Book the Room

Young talent usually needs current headshots, accurate measurements, a simple resume, parent contact information, and reliable self-tape access. Keep materials truthful and age-appropriate, and update them as your child grows.

Audition Logistics

Parents should be ready for fast turnarounds, self-tape instructions, wardrobe notes, location details, and availability checks. A shared calendar, quiet recording spot, and organized file naming can make the process smoother.

Family Boundaries

A healthy career starts with a healthy child. Decide in advance what your family can manage around school, sleep, travel, finances, social media, and rejection. The right opportunities should support the child, not consume the child.

Official Resources

Requirements can change, so parents should verify details with the issuing agency, school, payroll contact, union, or qualified professional for their specific situation.
